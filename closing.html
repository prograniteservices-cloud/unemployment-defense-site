Closing Argument

Summary

The employer has the burden of proving misconduct. They have failed to do so. Under South Carolina law, misconduct requires a willful or deliberate disregard of the employer's interests. The evidence shows the exact opposite: a dedicated employee who was systematically denied the tools, the help, and the time needed to succeed.

Key Arguments for Reversal

  • 1. Set Up to Fail: You cannot measure seams without a tape measure. I requested one for two months. You cannot hand-lift 800 sq ft of stone daily without the helper that was fired in August. These are "good faith errors" caused by lack of tools—protected under Regulation 47-101.
  • 2. Retaliation as Pretext: After months of zero write-ups, the disciplinary actions started on November 13th—the very day management learned of my plans to move. This proves the write-ups were not about performance, but about building a paper trail to fire a departing employee for cause.
  • 3. Systemic Failure: Andrew Bazil (Shop Manager) confirmed on Feb 11, 2026: "Nothing is leaving shop done right... Nobody knows shit." The quality issues did not leave with me; they are systemic to the shop's lack of leadership and functioning equipment.

Request:

I respectfully request that the determination be reversed and that unemployment benefits be granted, as my discharge was due to factors beyond my control and clearly not misconduct.

Thank you for your time and consideration.
