Opening Statement

Case: Barry Beaubien vs. Stonequarters, LLC

Good morning. My name is Barry Beaubien, and I am here today because I was wrongfully denied unemployment benefits.

I was discharged on December 5, 2025, for "Work Performance"—specifically, alleged "Inefficiency/Inability." Under South Carolina Regulation 47-101, a discharge resulting from substandard performance due to inefficiency, inability, or incapacity does not disqualify a claimant from unemployment benefits—especially when that failure was beyond the claimant's control, had no harmful intent, or was a good faith error in judgment. The employer bears the burden of proving misconduct, and I intend to show they cannot meet that burden.

I did not willfully disregard my employer's interests. On the contrary, I was a dedicated employee who worked 50–60+ hour weeks through June and July of 2025 to meet the demands of the shop. My managers were aware and appreciative of this effort.

What changed was not my work ethic—it was my working conditions. Specifically:

1. My employer refused to provide essential tools.

I requested a tape measure for two consecutive months. That request was ignored. I was then written up on December 4th for a "seam measurement" error—work that is impossible to do accurately without a measuring tool. I was finally handed a tape measure on December 5th—the day I was fired. I will present text messages proving I made these requests and that management's own response was, and I quote, "they don't care."

2. My workload tripled with no support.

In August 2025, my helper Nate was fired and never replaced. I was the only employee in my section required to hand-lift approximately 800 square feet of stone daily—roughly 15 jobs—because using the shared crane required pushing 1-ton carts, which my manager Robbie strictly forbade me from doing alone. On August 13th, I texted Robbie directly: "Do we have any plans to hire me a helper... now that Nate is gone?" No help ever came.

3. My ability to finish work was then eliminated.

On September 18th, a "No Overtime" notice was posted: "CLOCK OUT AT 5:00pm!" As the last person in the production line, I was receiving unfinished, defective work from prior stages late in the day. Overtime was the only way I could complete quality checks and corrections. That was taken away. On August 6th, I had warned Robbie in writing: "We need adequate time to do quality control." He replied, "I gotcha." Nothing changed.

4. The timing of the disciplinary actions proves retaliation.

I worked for months with zero write-ups. Then, on November 13th—the very day management asked me about my plans to move—I received my first write-up. Three weeks later, I was fired. I went from a valued employee to terminated in under four weeks, and it started the moment they thought I was leaving.

Today I will present text messages, photographs, and witness testimony that corroborate every one of these claims. I will also show you texts proving that since my departure, the shop's quality problems have continued—because the issues were never about me. They were systemic.

I respectfully ask that you find no misconduct and grant my unemployment benefits.
